About

Hello, my name is Logan Joyce. I am a Game Development Generalist who specializes in stylized 3D games. I am drawn to this style because I have always played on systems that usually cannot play realistic AAA games. To me games are about feeling good and having a place to have fun even for people who may not have the most optimal gaming setups. I have always been into video games. To me they are more than just something to do, they are something that I can engage with on my own and with my friends. As a military child I moved quite a few times, so being able to play with the friends I moved away from online has allowed me to stay close to people for much longer. Because of my interest in video games, when I was around 10 years old, I became interested in creating them. Over time, I learned more and more about the various parts of game development such as programming, 3D art, graphic design, UI design, level design, and more. As I continued my game development journey, I definitely had lots of failures due to scope, ambition, and just how hard it was to make a game. Like everyone, I had wanted to make these grand, hard to achieve ideas, but in the end I was humbled greatly. These lessons were very useful to my overall growth though because now I understand what it takes to make a game in all of its different skill sets. Although I will always be learning, I can be a valuable member of any game design team. In college I have been able to experience what it is like to work on games collaboratively via the Virginia Tech Game Development Club Game Jam, the Virginia Inter-collegiate Game Jam, and a Collaborative Game Production class. Through these experiences I have been able to see multiple games through from start to finish and how games are collaboratively made. My current focus is on becoming a Technical Artist. I want to help make more games accessible on lower-end systems and make cool shaders and tools. Currently I am based in Blacksburg, Virginia but I love travel and am open to remote work or relocation.
